What is HEIC?

HEIC is a container commonly used by Apple devices for HEIF images. It can preserve good visual quality at a smaller size than an equivalent JPEG, but older apps, websites, and non-Apple workflows do not always accept it.

What is JPG?

JPG is a lossy image format supported by nearly every browser, editor, messaging app, and upload form. It is best suited to photographs and other images with many colors or gradual tones.

When should I convert HEIC to JPG?

  • A website or form rejects an HEIC upload.
  • You need to send photos to someone using software without HEIC support.
  • A printing, editing, or document workflow expects JPG files.

Keep in mind: JPG does not support transparency. HEIC metadata is not copied into the converted file, and re-encoding may introduce a small quality loss.

Frequently asked questions

Will my HEIC photo be uploaded?

No. The HEIC decoder and JPG encoder run in a Web Worker on your device.

Can I convert several HEIC photos at once?

Yes. Add up to 20 files, convert them sequentially, then download individual JPG files or a ZIP.

Does conversion preserve EXIF metadata?

No. The current converter writes the decoded pixels to a new file and does not copy source metadata.

Why can JPG look slightly different?

JPG uses lossy compression. Choose a higher quality setting when preserving fine photographic detail matters.
